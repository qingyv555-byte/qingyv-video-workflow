# Motion direction

## Start with meaning

Give each shot one expression verb such as gather, weave, expand, trace, match, split, connect, or break. The subject motion should perform that verb. Support layers and camera movement should clarify it.

For product films, confirm the real capability before inventing the visual metaphor. A plausible-looking success state must not imply a feature or result the product does not provide.

## Design independent tracks

Separate these layers:

1. **Subject:** the state change that explains the idea.
2. **Support:** guides attention, establishes context, or shows cause and effect.
3. **Typography:** creates a reading window and a clear hierarchy.
4. **Camera:** controls distance, focus, and spatial continuity.
5. **Sound:** reinforces preparation, impact, transition, and result.

Position, scale, rotation, opacity, blur, color, path, and shape do not need one duration or easing curve. Define key states first, then tune the speed between them.

## Calibrate amplitude

Measure or specify:

- travel distance and bounding-box change as a percentage of frame size;
- angular range and change of orientation;
- deformation controls and topology changes;
- foreground/background separation and camera scale;
- the frame of maximum speed, maximum extent, reversal, and rest.

Check whether amplitude supports the intended hierarchy. A large subject change may require smaller support motion. A selected item may enlarge while alternatives retreat, dim, or become occluded.

## Organize time

- Use local shot time and explicit labels for reusable events.
- Overlap preparation, primary motion, follow-through, and the next transition when continuity benefits.
- Offset related elements instead of starting every property on the same frame.
- Keep a readable result state long enough to understand, especially when the visual transformation is fast.
- Treat music as a timing aid. Do not quantize every change to an identical half-second pulse.

## Distinguish kinds of motion

- **Internal deformation:** structure, control points, paths, or topology change.
- **Object motion:** the whole element translates, rotates, or scales.
- **Camera motion:** the view changes around the scene.

One cannot substitute for another. Rotating an unchanged symbol does not create weaving; zooming the camera does not create structural expansion.

## Avoid empty activity

Stillness can be intentional when it creates a reading or impact window. If the reference remains alive, do not leave the subject as a static pattern. Tiny jitter, looping breathing, particles, or random rotation cannot replace a meaningful state change.
